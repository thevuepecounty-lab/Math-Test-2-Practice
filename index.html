Before you begin

Each answer field is worth 1 mark. Questions with two answer fields earn partial credit. Unanswered fields earn zero. Every test contains 30 questions worth 45 marks: 9 from Week 5, 11 from Week 6, and 10 from Week 7.

  • Numbers: units are optional when the requested unit is shown in the answer label. Equivalent trailing zeros and fractions are accepted. Large numbers may be entered with no separator or with spaces between groups of three digits; for example, 1200000 or 1 200 000. Do not use commas in numerical answers. If you enter a unit, it must be correct or convertible to the requested unit.
  • Injectable rounding: below 1 mL, round to hundredths. Greater than 1 mL, round to tenths. An exact 1 mL requires no added decimal. Omit unnecessary trailing zeros.
  • Weight-based calculations: convert pounds to kilograms using 2.2 lb = 1 kg and round converted weight to the nearest tenth before continuing.
  • Theory: theory items are multiple choice or require only a one- or two-word answer. Capitalization, punctuation, common abbreviations, singular/plural forms, and minor spelling variations are accepted.

Useful frequencies: BID = 2 doses/day; TID or q8h = 3; QID or q6h = 4; q4h = 6; q3h = 8; q12h = 2.

The questions use the rules and ranges stated in the supplied course material. On the class insulin sliding scale, 4.1–7 mmol/L requires no insulin and a reading above 20 mmol/L requires a call to the prescriber.

Sources and question-bank notes

The bank contains 120 questions: 37 for Week 5, 37 for Week 6, and 46 for Week 7. Every generated test contains only four short theory questions and 26 dosage calculations.

  • Week 5 Oral Dosages: 9 questions / 14 marks. One mathematics-related theory question, three single-answer calculations, and five two-part calculations.
  • Week 6 Parenteral Dosages: 11 questions / 16 marks. One mathematics-related theory question, five single-answer calculations, and five two-part calculations.
  • Week 7 Units, Insulin, Heparin, and Weight-Based Dosages: 10 questions / 15 marks. Two brief safety/theory questions and eight calculation questions, including one five-part safe-dose-range calculation.

Week 5 sources: Oral Dosages Student; oral dosage answer keys; Dosage Calculations textbook, Chapters 13 and 15.

Week 6 sources: Parenteral Dosages NEW; parenteral practice questions and answer keys; Dosage Calculations textbook, Chapter 16.

Week 7 sources: Dosages Measured in Units 2022; dosage-in-units and weight-based practice materials; Dosage Calculations textbook, Chapters 18 and 21. Five adapted safe-dose-range questions form a dedicated pool, and exactly one appears on each test.
